# Access Controls on a Mobile Device — Artifact 0.1 AI Assistance Disclosure: This experiment was primarily self-directed. AI assistance was used to refine the written explanation and, during troubleshooting, may have contributed to the idea of using Modes and Routines to address concerns I identified. I performed the configurations and testing and drew the conclusions documented here.     This artifact demonstrates how I applied layered access controls on my personal mobile device to balance security, privacy, and day‑to‑day usability. Because the legal protections surrounding compelled biometric device access in the United States remain unsettled and can vary by jurisdiction, I shifted my device’s primary unlock method from biometrics to a password. This increases the difficulty of unauthorized access, especially in public or high‑risk environments.     To maintain usability, I enabled the device’s Extend Unlock feature. This feature modifies lock‑screen behavior under defined conditions, either bypassing authentication or changing the required authentication method. The first unlock always requires the primary credential (my password). For the next four hours, the device continuously evaluates whether the conditions for Extend Unlock are still met. When they are, I can use my fingerprint for quick access.     I configured these conditions to reflect real‑world access patterns. In certain trusted locations, Extend Unlock requires both my fingerprint and the presence of my smartwatch, which functions as a physical key. In other locations, only the fingerprint is required. Together, these configurations apply conditional-access principles using factors such as passwords, location, Wi-Fi, and possession of an additional device.     These controls allow me to access my phone quickly when I need to—such as when looking up information for a customer at work—while still maintaining strong protections against unauthorized access. This artifact illustrates how identity signals, environmental context, and device‑based factors can be combined to create a secure and usable access control strategy.
